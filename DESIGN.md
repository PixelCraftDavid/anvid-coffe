# Anvid Coffe visual direction

An intimate editorial world: deep olive, black edges, liquid glass and afternoon light. The entire experience is one centred game window, followed by its result and a button to review the coffee.

- Paper: #f6f4ed. Ink: #292b24. Olive: #444b32. Muted olive: #737e52.
- Display: Instrument Serif, regular and italic. Interface: DM Sans.
- On full page entry, fine ivory/gold linework traces an original geometric moka pot and cup over the existing olive-black palette. A finite 3.2-second drawing/pour/fill/steam sequence dissolves in 260ms, with immediate skip/Escape and an optional replay in the demo controls. It is focus-managed and bypassed for reduced motion. Original liquid foley shares its visual timeline: a delicate stream, fuller cup splash and two settling drips. Audio is baked locally and decoded asynchronously to preserve the animation’s fluidity. Two restrained 44px controls provide sound activation/silence and skip, without changing the centred artwork. A first-visit autoplay block leaves audio off until the visitor activates it; late activation follows the current phase. This is a brief brand welcome, not a simulated network progress indicator.
- One card centred in the viewport. No landing page, marketing sections or game modal. The user explicitly requested this sequence.
- The supplied Egypt reference sets the background composition: monumental uppercase “ANVID” is a decorative type layer, partially occluded by a centred photographic cup, with the game window foremost. The lid peeks above the glass. Separate anchor/float/celebration transforms preserve centring through continuous motion and winning spins. The foreground window is compact (380px wide on desktop, 350px on shorter desktop screens, 328px on phones) and bounded by the available viewport height. Compact spacing and a smaller headline let the scratch view fit without page scrolling, exposing more of the cup and lettering. Redundant tiny signature copy is hidden; primary reveal and action targets remain 44px tall. Dense content can scroll inside the bounded window when necessary.
- The user requested stronger curvature, liquid glass, moving coffee photography, green light around the coffee fading smoothly into black at the edges, subtle grain and flowing colour.
- Glass uses 29–36px corners, a fine specular rim, inset highlights, translucent olive and a bounded backdrop blur. Child controls have soft 14–20px corners.
- A low-resolution WebGL flow field supplies olive light and static grain at 30 fps. It pauses in hidden tabs and renders a static frame for reduced motion. CSS animates the photo and ambient light layers using transforms and opacity, with a CSS fallback when WebGL is unavailable.
- The paper cup is a separate alpha image over a cleaned background plate. It continuously floats and tilts. A win spins it 360 degrees, shrinks it, then lands with a spring (duration 0.8, bounce 0.3). Photographic beans pass behind the glass to make its depth visible. The material uses 12–15px blur, boosted saturation, thin specular rims and contrast-aware light text.
- The glass tint is dark olive over the central ivory cup, keeping the light headline and coffee description readable. The cleaned background plate is subdued and centred so it does not create a competing product on the right. Wordmark → cup → glass is an explicit stacking order; decorative layers ignore pointer events and assistive technology.
- The card material has independent optical, centre-frost, tint and specular layers behind the UI. Chromium uses a generated rounded-rectangle displacement field to refract the live backdrop through a curved outer shoulder and inner lip. The reading centre stays neutral. Moving objects warp continuously as they cross this fixed optical profile; no turbulence or extra animation loop moves the content. The lens is baked at local layout size, ignoring Motion's presentation scaling, and updated after size changes settle. Other engines keep a layered frosted fallback; transparency/contrast preferences retain the opaque alternative.
- Canvas removes foil under pointer input; coverage unlocks the result. Motion provides short state transitions and success feedback. Review focus moves to the next step's title.
- Strong ease-out: cubic-bezier(.23,1,.32,1); UI transitions 150–250ms. Pointer spring duration 0.5, bounce 0.2.
- Reduced motion removes parallax, rotation, marquee and confetti. Touch gets direct scratch input and no sticky hover transforms.
- Responsive at 620px, 800px and 1100px, with touch-safe controls, safe-area dialog padding and accessible alternative reveal.

References inspected: https://motion.dev, https://21st.dev, https://skiper-ui.com, https://horizonx.so, https://animmasterlib.dev. Motion is the installed runtime. Components are authored for Anvid rather than copying paid component code.
